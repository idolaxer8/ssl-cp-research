\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Frozen self-supervised representations.}
Vision transformers~\citep{dosovitskiy2021image} are increasingly used as general-purpose foundation models, with large-scale pre-training producing transferable visual representations. DINOv2 and its successors provide features that support lightweight probes across tasks without fine-tuning~\citep{oquab2023dinov2, simeoni2025dinov3}, while CLIP learns transferable image embeddings from language supervision~\citep{radford2021clip}. FRCP follows this paradigm with a frozen backbone and nearest-prototype prediction, evaluated on DINOv2 and CLIP.

\paragraph{Representation refinement.}
Post-hoc refinement of pre-trained descriptors is a long-standing lever
in image retrieval: principal component analysis (PCA) with whitening suppresses co-occurrence
structure and improves matching~\citep{jegou2012negative}, and the
practice persists in current training-free systems, where
\citet{min2026street} refine DINOv2 embeddings with PCA-based whitening
for zero-shot cross-view retrieval. Few-shot classification converged on
the same operation: Simple CNAPS shows that a regularized Mahalanobis
metric, that is, whitened distances, outperforms Euclidean and cosine
comparisons~\citep{bateni2020improved}. These findings transfer to
conformal prediction for a simple reason: a training-free nonconformity
score is itself a retrieval-style computation, ranking candidate classes
by embedding distance. Thus, whatever sharpens distances for retrieval and
nearest-class classification also sharpens the score's separation of
true from false labels. Our approach imports truncation and whitening as its
first two refinement stages, fitted on the unlabeled pool.

\paragraph{Conformal prediction.}
CP turns any score into prediction sets with finite-sample coverage,
assuming only exchangeability of calibration and test
data~\citep{vovk2005algorithmic, angelopoulos2023gentle}. In its
original, full (transductive) form, each candidate label $k$ for a test
point $x$ is scored after augmenting the calibration bag with the pair
$(x, k)$, all $n{+}1$ scores are computed symmetrically, and $k$ enters
the prediction set when its rank-based p-value exceeds
$\alpha$~\citep{vovk2005algorithmic}. With $n$ labeled examples, the
resulting set satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:coverage}
\Pr\!\left[\, y \in \Gamma_\alpha(x) \,\right] \;\ge\; 1 - \alpha
\end{equation}
for the test pair $(x, y)$ at any sample size, with a matching upper
bound of $1 - \alpha + \tfrac{1}{n+1}$ when ties occur with probability
zero. Split
CP~\citep{papadopoulos2002inductive, lei2018distribution} trades this
construction for computation: a model is fitted once on a training split
and only a threshold is calibrated on the rest, which made CP practical
at scale and made THR, APS, and RAPS the standard classification
scores~\citep{sadinle2019least, romano2020aps, angelopoulos2021raps}.
Full CP has remained attractive wherever its cost can be tamed, a
lineage that begins with the closed-form ridge regression confidence
machine~\citep{nouretdinov2001ridge} and nearest-neighbor
scores~\citep{vovk2005algorithmic} and continues in exact accelerations
of full CP through incremental and decremental
learning~\citep{cherubin2021exact}. We extends this lineage, combining full CP with a training-free score at modest computational cost.

\paragraph{Conformal prediction with few labels or unlabeled data.}
The label-scarce regime has been approached from three directions.
\citet{fisch2021few} meta-learn nonconformity scores from auxiliary
tasks so that few in-task labels suffice. SemiCP enlarges the
calibration set itself, assigning unlabeled points
nearest-neighbor-matched scores~\citep{zhou2026semicp}. Closest to our
work, FCA runs full CP transductively over a few-shot adaptation set to
jointly adapt and conformalize medical vision-language
models~\citep{silva2025full}. FRCP shares its full-CP thesis but remains training-free and works at
a few labels per class, where fitting even a linear probe strains the
budget. Without the text tower its adaptation starts from, FCA
degenerates in our setting to full CP under a prototype-style score on
unrefined embeddings, which is the raw arm of the score ablation
(Appendix~\ref{app:ablation}). A separate line smooths
nonconformity scores over graph neighborhoods: DAPS diffuses scores
along a given graph, and SNAPS extends the aggregation to
feature-similar nodes~\citep{zargarbashi2023conformal,
song2024similarity}. Our smoothing stage is the representation-level
counterpart of this idea: descended from query expansion in
retrieval~\citep{radenovic2019finetuning}, it averages each embedding
with its unlabeled-pool neighbors before any score exists, rather than
correcting scores afterwards.
