\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

Let $\phi$ denote an encoder pre-trained without labels from the target task, fixed throughout the procedure, mapping images to $\mathbb{R}^d$. We work directly in its
embedding space and write $x = \phi(a) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ for the
representation of image $a$. We observe a labeled sample
$\mathcal{D}_n = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$ with $y_i \in [K] :=
\{1, \dots, K\}$, an unlabeled pool $\mathcal{U} = \{u_j\}_{j=1}^{m}$
drawn from the same image distribution and independent of
$\mathcal{D}_n$, and a test embedding $x_{n+1}$ with hidden label
$y_{n+1}$. Our only distributional assumption is that
$(x_1, y_1), \dots, (x_{n+1}, y_{n+1})$ are exchangeable. For a target
miscoverage level $\alpha \in (0,1)$, we seek a set-valued predictor
that decides, for every candidate label $k \in [K]$, whether $k$ enters
the prediction set $\Gamma_\alpha(x_{n+1})$, so that the coverage
requirement~\eqref{eq:coverage} holds for the pair
$(x_{n+1}, y_{n+1})$, while keeping the sets as close to singletons as
the coverage constraint permits.
CP builds such predictors from a nonconformity score $S(x, k)$, a
real-valued measure of how poorly the pair $(x, k)$ conforms to the
labeled data. The choice of score governs efficiency, while coverage
comes from the calibration construction. Given scores
$S_i = S(x_i, y_i)$ on a calibration sample of size $n_{\mathrm{cal}}$
drawn from the labeled data, the threshold is the inflated empirical
quantile
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:quantile}
\hat q_\alpha \;=\; S_{(\lceil (1-\alpha)(n_{\mathrm{cal}}+1) \rceil)} ,
\end{equation}
the $\lceil (1-\alpha)(n_{\mathrm{cal}}+1) \rceil$-th smallest
calibration score, and the prediction set collects every candidate that
scores no worse:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:set}
\Gamma_\alpha(x_{n+1}) \;=\;
\bigl\{\, k \in [K] \;:\; S(x_{n+1}, k) \le \hat q_\alpha \,\bigr\} .
\end{equation}
Exchangeability of the calibration and test pairs then implies
\eqref{eq:coverage}~\citep{vovk2005algorithmic,
papadopoulos2002inductive}.

\textbf{The cost of a training split.} In the standard split pipeline
the score comes from a trained model: the labeled data are divided as
$n = n_{\mathrm{tr}} + n_{\mathrm{cal}}$, a $K$-way classifier is
fitted on the first part, and \eqref{eq:quantile} and \eqref{eq:set}
are computed on the second. Writing $s = n/K$ for the average number
of labels per class, our regime of interest is small $s$, where both
portions are starved at once and reducing either has a direct cost.
With few training examples, the classifier separates the correct class
from the $K-1$ alternatives only weakly, so the sets stay valid but wide.
Increasing the training portion does not solve
the problem for free, because it simultaneously shrinks the calibration
sample, and small $n_{\mathrm{cal}}$ carries its own penalty:
conditional on the calibration draw, coverage follows a Beta
distribution with variance approximately
$\alpha(1-\alpha)/(n_{\mathrm{cal}}+2)$, and \citet{zhou2026semicp}
analyze precisely this limited-label instability, quantifying
non-negligible probabilities of severe under-coverage at small
calibration sizes alongside heavier-tailed set sizes.
Class-conditional variants are stricter still, requiring at least
$\lceil 1/\alpha \rceil - 1$ calibration labels per class (nine at
$\alpha = 0.1$), beyond reach when $s$ is small and the same labels
must also train the classifier. At small $s$ no allocation escapes the bind, cross-validation hybrids included~\citep{barber2021predictive}: every split starves one side or the other.

\textbf{Full conformal prediction.} Full CP~\citep{vovk2005algorithmic}
removes the split. For each candidate label
$k \in [K]$, the labeled sample is augmented with the hypothesized pair
$(x_{n+1}, k)$ and all $n{+}1$ points are scored symmetrically, so the
entire labeled sample does calibration work. Removing the split pays off only
if the score itself needs no training, which is what a general-purpose backbone provides: a representation where candidate labels can be scored from the labeled points directly.
The framework also disciplines method design: to keep the augmented sample symmetric,
every label-dependent quantity must be recomputed for each of the $K$
candidate labels of every test point, so label-dependent machinery
multiplies computation by $K$, whereas label-free components are fitted
once and shared across all candidates.

These observations separate three resources, each supplying what the others cannot. The frozen encoder gives a representation at no label cost. The unlabeled pool can be far larger ($m \gg n$)
and what it adds is geometry: the structure, variance, and local density of that representation, which is what the refinement uses. The labeled sample is then reserved, in its entirety, for conformal calibration.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Pool-fitted representation refinement}
\label{sec:refine}

The refinement map
$T_{\mathcal{U}} : \mathbb{R}^{d} \to \mathbb{R}^{d'}$ is constructed
from the unlabeled pool alone and applied to every embedding,
$z = T_{\mathcal{U}}(x)$. Labeled points and the test point pass
through the same map. It composes three label-free stages,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pipeline}
T_{\mathcal{U}} \;=\; T_{\mathrm{smo}} \circ T_{\mathrm{wht}} \circ
T_{\mathrm{trc}} ,
\end{equation}
each fitted on $\mathcal{U}$ and each addressing a distinct defect of
the raw embeddings. Which stages are active, and the few constants each
one takes, are fixed per dataset from pool statistics, before any
labeled data are seen.

\textbf{Truncation.} With pool mean
$\mu_{\mathcal{U}} = \frac{1}{m}\sum_j u_j$ and pool covariance
$\widehat\Sigma_{\mathcal{U}} = \frac{1}{m}\sum_j (u_j -
\mu_{\mathcal{U}})(u_j - \mu_{\mathcal{U}})^{\top}$, let
$V \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d'}$ collect the leading $d'$ eigenvectors
of $\widehat\Sigma_{\mathcal{U}}$. The first stage is the centered
projection $T_{\mathrm{trc}}(x) = V^{\top}(x - \mu_{\mathcal{U}})$,
PCA fitted on the pool. Its purpose is not compression. The pool measures its low-variance directions poorly, and the next stage divides by that measurement, so error in those directions would be magnified rather than removed. \citet{jegou2012negative} report exactly this in retrieval, where whitening a descriptor at full dimension amplifies the noise on its low-energy components and costs accuracy. Truncation removes those directions first, leaving whitening a subspace the pool measures well.

\textbf{Whitening.} $T_{\mathrm{wht}}$ rescales the truncated space, and
the question is which covariance to rescale by. A distance-based score
reads variation between classes, while variation inside a class, from
pose or lighting, is nuisance. Dividing by the pool's total covariance
would flatten both, so only the within-class part is standardized.
Labels are unavailable, so $k$-means groups stand in for classes: the
pool projections are partitioned into $C$ groups and one covariance is
pooled from every point's deviation from its own group mean,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmaw}
\widehat\Sigma_{\mathrm{w}} \;=\; \frac{1}{m} \sum_{g=1}^{C}
\sum_{j \in G_g} (t_j - \bar t_g)(t_j - \bar t_g)^{\top} ,
\end{equation}
with $G_g$ the $g$-th group and $\bar t_g$ its mean. One shared estimate
draws on all $m$ points rather than the few in any single group. The
stage is the rescaling
$T_{\mathrm{wht}}(t) = \widehat\Sigma_{\mathrm{w}}^{-1/2}\, t$, which is
invertible: distances are read in units of within-class spread.
Euclidean distance in the rescaled space is Mahalanobis distance under
$\widehat\Sigma_{\mathrm{w}}$, the metric that few-shot classification
and training-free retrieval both settle on for frozen
embeddings~\citep{bateni2020improved, min2026street}.

\textbf{Smoothing.} $T_{\mathrm{smo}}$ averages each whitened
embedding with its nearest pool neighbors. Write $v$ for a whitened embedding, normalized to unit length. The pool is carried through the same two stages and normalized the same way, and $N_{\kappa}(v)$ collects the $\kappa$ pool points of largest cosine with $v$, written $u$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:qe}
T_{\mathrm{smo}}(v) \;=\;
\frac{v + \sum_{u \in N_{\kappa}(v)} w_u\, u}
     {\bigl\| v + \sum_{u \in N_{\kappa}(v)} w_u\, u \bigr\|},
\qquad
w_u = \max\{\cos(v, u),\, 0\}^{\gamma} ,
\end{equation}
where the exponent $\gamma$ concentrates weight on the closest
neighbors and the truncation at zero discards those pointing away from
$v$. The point itself enters at unit weight, so the operation corrects
an embedding rather than replacing it by its neighborhood, and the
closing normalization is free because the score reads cosines.

The operator is weighted query expansion from image
retrieval~\citep{radenovic2019finetuning}, and the reason to want it in
a conformal pipeline comes from score smoothing on graphs: DAPS and
SNAPS shrink each node's nonconformity score toward the scores of its
neighbors, and the sets tighten when neighborhoods are
label-homogeneous~\citep{zargarbashi2023conformal, song2024similarity}.
FRCP performs the same averaging one level earlier, on the
representation rather than the score, which is what allows it to
precede any hypothesized label. The saving is structural. A correction
carried by the embedding is computed once per point, whereas smoothing
the score itself would repeat for each of the $K$ candidates
(Section~\ref{sec:background}). The order of the stages is likewise
forced: smoothing consumes neighborhoods, and a neighborhood is worth
only as much as the metric that selects it, which the two preceding
stages have just repaired.

\subsection{Full conformal prediction with a training-free prototype score}
\label{sec:fcp}

After refinement, each labeled example is represented as
$z_i = T_{\mathcal{U}}(x_i)$ and the test point as
$z_{n+1} = T_{\mathcal{U}}(x_{n+1})$. For a candidate label
$k \in [K]$, form the augmented sample
$(z_1, y_1), \dots, (z_n, y_n), (z_{n+1}, k)$ and write $\ell_i$ for
the label of point $i$ in it, so $\ell_i = y_i$ for $i \le n$ and
$\ell_{n+1} = k$. Let $\mu^{(k, -i)}_{c}$ denote the mean of the
refined embeddings carrying label $c$ in the augmented sample after
removing point $i$. Each point is scored against these leave-one-out
prototypes through a temperature-scaled softmax over cosine
similarities,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:score}
S^{(k)}_{i} \;=\;
1 - \frac{\exp\bigl(\cos(z_i,\, \mu^{(k,-i)}_{\ell_i}) / T\bigr)}
         {\sum_{c=1}^{K} \exp\bigl(\cos(z_i,\, \mu^{(k,-i)}_{c}) / T\bigr)} ,
\end{equation}
so a point conforms when it lies close to the prototype of its own
label and far from the others. The temperature carries weight here.
Cosine similarities are confined to $[-1, 1]$, so an untempered
softmax over them is nearly flat and the resulting score separates
candidates only weakly. We fix $T$ per dataset and report the values
in Section~5.

Two updates are at work, each touching one class. Inserting
$(z_{n+1}, k)$ moves the prototype of class $k$ alone, and removing
point $i$ moves the prototype of $\ell_i$ alone, so every other
$\mu^{(k,-i)}_{c}$ is the plain class mean of the labeled sample.
% CUT (user call 08-26): the empty-class convention. The implementation
% carries it -- if leave-one-out empties a point's own class the
% prototype is undefined, the point takes the maximal score, and the
% same rule fires for cal and test points so symmetry survives -- but
% the reported protocol is balanced with s >= 2 (grid s in
% {2,4,...,14}, experiments.tex L120), so at s = 2 leave-one-out still
% leaves one point in the class and the rule never fires. Documenting a
% branch no reported run reaches costs three sentences and invites the
% reviewer to picture a degenerate regime. Restore only if an
% unbalanced-split or s = 1 experiment enters the paper.
The conformal p-value of candidate $k$ compares the test score with
all others,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pvalue}
p_k \;=\; \frac{1}{n+1} \sum_{i=1}^{n+1}
\mathbf{1}\bigl\{ S^{(k)}_{i} \ge S^{(k)}_{n+1} \bigr\} ,
\qquad
\Gamma_\alpha(x_{n+1}) \;=\; \bigl\{\, k \in [K] : p_k > \alpha \,\bigr\} .
\end{equation}
Under exchangeability of the labeled and test pairs, this construction
satisfies \eqref{eq:coverage} at any finite $n$~\citep{vovk2005algorithmic}.

\textbf{Computational cost.} Full CP is usually set aside as
impractical because every candidate label demands a refit. For the
prototype score that refit is closed form. Prototypes are linear in
the data, so the leave-one-out update is
$\mu^{(-i)}_{c} = (n_c \mu_c - z_i)/(n_c - 1)$, with no inverse to
recompute and no rank-one correction to propagate. Folding
$(z_{n+1}, k)$ into the sample moves only the class-$k$ prototype, so
exactly one column of the cached calibration logit matrix changes.
Only the class-$k$ term of each calibration point's softmax denominator
moves, so $D \leftarrow D - exp_{\mathrm{old}} + exp_{\mathrm{new}}$, at $O(n)$
per candidate rather than $O(nK)$.

The work then falls into three tiers. Once for the whole run, the class
sums and the calibration logits are formed in $O(nKd')$. Once per test
point, its inner products against the calibration sample are formed in
$O(nd')$, and this is all the geometry the candidates need, since
folding the test point into class $k$ shifts that prototype by a
multiple of $z_{n+1}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:cost}). The sweep over candidates is then arithmetic on
scalars, $O(nK)$ for all $K$ together, with each p-value a comparison
count rather than a sort. Conformalizing one test point costs
$O(n(d' + K))$, against the $K$ refits a trained score would require.
Candidates and test points batch into matrix products on a GPU.
Every quantity above scales with $n$, so the per-test-point cost stays
small precisely because the labeled sample is small. Split CP
calibrates once and then answers each test point in time independent of
$n$, an advantage that grows with a budget this method does not target.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{FRCP: pool-fitted refinement + full conformal prediction}
\label{alg:frcp}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require pool $\mathcal{U}$, labeled sample $\mathcal{D}_n$, test point
$x_{n+1}$, level $\alpha$, configuration $(d', C, \kappa, \gamma)$,
temperature $T$
\Statex \textbf{Stage 1: fit $T_{\mathcal{U}}$ on the pool (label-free, once)}
\State $\mu_{\mathcal{U}} \gets \operatorname{mean}(\mathcal{U})$,
       $V \gets$ leading $d'$ eigenvectors of $\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{U})$
       \Comment{truncation}
\State $t_j \gets V^{\top}(u_j - \mu_{\mathcal{U}})$ for all $u_j \in \mathcal{U}$
\State $\widehat\Sigma_{\mathrm{w}} \gets$ pooled within-group covariance
       of $\{t_j\}$ over $C$ $k$-means groups, \eqref{eq:sigmaw}
\State $\tilde t_j \gets \widehat\Sigma_{\mathrm{w}}^{-1/2} t_j \,\big/\,
       \bigl\| \widehat\Sigma_{\mathrm{w}}^{-1/2} t_j \bigr\|$ for all $j$
       \Comment{whitening}
\State $T_{\mathcal{U}}(x) \gets T_{\mathrm{smo}}\bigl(\widehat\Sigma_{\mathrm{w}}^{-1/2}
       V^{\top}(x - \mu_{\mathcal{U}})\bigr)$ by \eqref{eq:qe}
       \Comment{smoothing}
\Statex \textbf{Stage 2: full conformal prediction on the refined embeddings}
\State $z_i \gets T_{\mathcal{U}}(x_i)$ for $i = 1, \dots, n$, and
       $z_{n+1} \gets T_{\mathcal{U}}(x_{n+1})$
\State $F_{ic} \gets \cos\bigl(z_i, \mu^{(-i)}_{c}\bigr)$ for all
       $i \in [n]$, $c \in [K]$
       \Comment{once per labeled sample}
\State $g_i \gets \langle z_{n+1},\, z_i \rangle$ for $i = 1, \dots, n$
       \Comment{once per test point}
\For{each candidate label $k \in [K]$}
  \State rebuild column $k$ of $F$ from $g$, folding in $(z_{n+1}, k)$
  \State $S^{(k)}_i \gets$ \eqref{eq:score} at temperature $T$, for all $i \le n{+}1$
  \State $p_k \gets$ \eqref{eq:pvalue}
\EndFor
\State \Return $\Gamma_\alpha(x_{n+1}) = \{k \in [K] : p_k > \alpha\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Theoretical analysis}
\label{sec:theory}
\begin{proposition}[Refinement preserves validity]
\label{prop:validity}
Let $T_{\mathcal{U}}$ be any measurable function of the pool
$\mathcal{U}$, with $\mathcal{U}$ independent of
$(x_1, y_1), \dots, (x_{n+1}, y_{n+1})$. If these pairs are
exchangeable, then conditional on $\mathcal{U}$ the transformed pairs
$(T_{\mathcal{U}}(x_1), y_1), \dots, (T_{\mathcal{U}}(x_{n+1}),
y_{n+1})$ are exchangeable, and the prediction set of
\eqref{eq:pvalue} satisfies \eqref{eq:coverage}.
\end{proposition}

The proof (Appendix~A) is short: conditional on $\mathcal{U}$,
$T_{\mathcal{U}}$ is a fixed map applied to every coordinate of an
exchangeable sequence, which preserves exchangeability, and the full
conformal guarantee holds conditionally on $\mathcal{U}$, hence also
marginally. The proposition asks nothing about the quality of the
refinement: $T_{\mathcal{U}}$ may be arbitrarily ill-suited to the
task and \eqref{eq:coverage} still holds. Whether refinement helps is
decided entirely in the geometry, which is where the next result
operates.

For the smoothing stage, drop the final normalization in
\eqref{eq:qe}, which the cosine score ignores, and consider smoothing
with a generic neighborhood weight,
$\hat z = (1-\beta)\, v + \beta\, \nu(v)$, where
$\nu(v) = \sum_{u \in N_{\kappa}(v)} (w_u / W)\, u$ and
$W = \sum_{u} w_u$. The deployed map \eqref{eq:qe} corresponds to the
implicit weight $\beta = W/(1+W)$. Fix any reference centers
$\{\theta_c\}_{c \in [K]}$ in the whitened space, for instance the
population class means, and suppose each pool neighbor $u$ carries an
unknown class $c(u)$. For a point
$v$ with class $y$, define its displacement
$\varepsilon = \lVert v - \theta_y \rVert$, each neighbor's own
displacement $r_u = \lVert u - \theta_{c(u)} \rVert$, the weighted
homophily $h = \frac{1}{W} \sum_{u : c(u) = y} w_u$, and the foreign
spread $\Delta(v) = \max_{u : c(u) \neq y} \lVert \theta_{c(u)} -
\theta_y \rVert$.

\begin{lemma}[When smoothing helps]
\label{lem:smooth}
For any $\beta \in (0, 1]$ and any nonnegative weights, the smoothed
point $\hat z = (1-\beta)\, v + \beta\, \nu(v)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:d1}
\lVert \hat z - \theta_y \rVert \;\le\;
(1-\beta)\, \varepsilon \;+\;
\beta \Bigl[\, \textstyle\sum_{u} \frac{w_u}{W}\, r_u
\;+\; (1 - h)\, \Delta(v) \,\Bigr] .
\end{equation}
In particular, $\lVert \hat z - \theta_y \rVert < \varepsilon$
whenever
$\sum_{u} \frac{w_u}{W}\, r_u + (1 - h)\, \Delta(v) < \varepsilon$.
\end{lemma}

The bound is a triangle inequality, proved in Appendix~A. It is
deterministic, holding conditional on the realized embeddings with no
distributional assumptions. The sufficient condition has a simple
geometric interpretation. The current displacement $\varepsilon$ must
exceed the sum of two terms: the average neighborhood displacement
from the relevant class centers, and a penalty proportional to the
neighborhood's foreign-weight fraction. When this condition holds,
smoothing provably decreases the distance to $\theta_y$. The condition
is sufficient only, and where it fails an impure enough neighborhood
can move $v$ farther instead. The guarantee concerns the
representation, not prediction-set size.

The lemma is the representation-space analogue of the guarantee behind
score diffusion~\citep{zargarbashi2023conformal}. What is assumed there,
that neighbors carry close ground truth, appears here as the weighted
homophily $h$, a property of the realized neighborhood rather than a
quantity the method estimates. The pool is unlabeled, so $h$ is never
computed at run time and the condition is a diagnostic rather than a
test the pipeline can apply.
