\section{Proofs}

\subsection*{Proposition~\ref{prop:validity}}

Fix a realization $\mathcal{U} = u$. Because $\mathcal{U}$ is
independent of $(x_1, y_1), \dots, (x_{n+1}, y_{n+1})$, the conditional
law of that sequence given $\mathcal{U} = u$ is its unconditional law,
which is exchangeable by assumption. Conditional on $\mathcal{U} = u$
the refinement map $T_u$ is a fixed measurable function
$\mathbb{R}^{d} \to \mathbb{R}^{d'}$, applied to one point at a time.
The smoothing stage evaluates each point against the frozen pool alone,
so no labeled or test point enters the transform of any other, and
$T_u$ is a per-point map rather than a function of the bag.

Let $\pi$ be a permutation of $[n+1]$. Applying a per-point map
commutes with permutation, so
\[
\bigl(T_u(x_{\pi(1)}), y_{\pi(1)}\bigr), \dots,
\bigl(T_u(x_{\pi(n+1)}), y_{\pi(n+1)}\bigr)
\]
is the image under $T_u$ of the permuted sequence. The permuted
sequence has the same law as the original, and equality in
distribution is preserved by a fixed measurable map, so the
transformed pairs are exchangeable conditional on $\mathcal{U} = u$.

The scores $S^{(k)}_i$ of \eqref{eq:score} are a symmetric function of
the augmented sample, since the leave-one-out prototypes treat every
index alike. The full conformal argument therefore applies conditional
on $\mathcal{U} = u$: the rank of the test score among the $n+1$ scores
is uniform on $\{1, \dots, n+1\}$, so
$\Pr[\, y_{n+1} \in \Gamma_\alpha(x_{n+1}) \mid \mathcal{U} = u \,]
\ge 1 - \alpha$. Averaging over the law of $\mathcal{U}$ gives
\eqref{eq:coverage}. \qed

\subsection*{Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth}}

Write $\hat z = (1-\beta)\, v + \beta\, \nu(v)$ with
$\nu(v) = \sum_{u} (w_u / W)\, u$ and $W = \sum_u w_u$, so the weights
$w_u / W$ sum to one. Then
\[
\nu(v) - \theta_y
= \sum_{u} \tfrac{w_u}{W} \bigl(u - \theta_{c(u)}\bigr)
+ \sum_{u} \tfrac{w_u}{W} \bigl(\theta_{c(u)} - \theta_y\bigr).
\]
The first sum is bounded in norm by
$\sum_u \tfrac{w_u}{W}\, r_u$. In the second, every term with
$c(u) = y$ vanishes, and each remaining term satisfies
$\lVert \theta_{c(u)} - \theta_y \rVert \le \Delta(v)$ by definition of
the foreign spread. Their total weight is
$\sum_{u : c(u) \neq y} w_u / W = 1 - h$, so
\[
\lVert \nu(v) - \theta_y \rVert \;\le\;
\sum_{u} \tfrac{w_u}{W}\, r_u \;+\; (1 - h)\, \Delta(v) .
\]
Applying the triangle inequality to
$\hat z - \theta_y = (1-\beta)(v - \theta_y) + \beta(\nu(v) - \theta_y)$
and using $\lVert v - \theta_y \rVert = \varepsilon$ gives
\eqref{eq:d1}. If the bracketed term is smaller than $\varepsilon$ and
$\beta \in (0, 1]$, then
$(1-\beta)\varepsilon + \beta \cdot (\text{bracket}) <
(1-\beta)\varepsilon + \beta \varepsilon = \varepsilon$, which is the
strict statement. \qed

\paragraph{The deployed weight.} The numerator of \eqref{eq:qe} is
\[
v + \sum_u w_u\, u
= (1 + W) \Bigl[ \tfrac{1}{1+W}\, v + \tfrac{W}{1+W}\, \nu(v) \Bigr] ,
\]
a positive multiple of $\hat z$ at $\beta = W / (1 + W)$. The cosine
score is invariant to positive rescaling of its first argument, so the
normalization in \eqref{eq:qe} leaves the score unchanged and the lemma
applies to the deployed map at that $\beta$.

\paragraph{Homophily and the Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth} certified fraction.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth} is a per-point sufficient condition, and
Table~\ref{tab:h-cert} instantiates it on the deployed pipeline. The weighted
pool-$k$NN homophily $h_w$, the same quantity Eq.~\eqref{eq:qe} weights, rises
under truncation and whitening on every dataset, most where it starts lowest
(from $0.81$ to $0.83$ on CIFAR-100), so whitening improves the
neighbourhoods the smoothing stage averages over. The certified fraction, the
share of labeled points for which the budget of the lemma falls below the
displacement $\varepsilon$, rises with it on three of the four datasets (from
$0.42$ to $0.52$ on CIFAR-100). It falls on EuroSAT ($0.45$ to $0.24$), the one
off-domain dataset, where whitening spreads the class centers apart faster
than it lowers the foreign weight $1 - h_w$, and we report this rather than
explain it away. The condition is sufficient only and
conservative: the realised fraction of points that move closer to their class
center after smoothing is near one on every dataset, so the certification
tracks where smoothing is provably safe rather than where it happens to help.

\section{Baseline implementations}
\label{app:baselines}

\paragraph{Baseline scores and probes.} Split CP and SemiCP score a
multinomial logistic probe on standardized features, fitted with the
same regularization for every method and budget, so differences between
them come from the conformal construction rather than from the head.
Both divide the labeled budget at a train fraction in
$\{0.25, 0.5, 0.75\}$, and we report the best cell per budget.
Baselines consume L2-normalized raw backbone embeddings, which is the
published form of each method, and the unlabeled pool is available to
every method able to use one.

\paragraph{SemiCP.} We follow the official implementation with one
documented change. The published method assumes a fully trained
classifier whose scores the nearest-neighbor matching then transfers to
unlabeled points, and no such classifier exists at our budgets. We
substitute the few-shot probe above, which is the same head the split
CP arm uses. This is the adaptation their own analysis anticipates,
since they report that matching quality tracks classifier accuracy.

\section{Attributing the gain to full CP, not to the score}
\label{app:attrib}

Every baseline in Table~\ref{tab:headline-main} differs from FRCP in both the
conformal construction and the score. Table~\ref{tab:attrib-a01} removes that
confound: all three rows use the identical frozen transform and frozen score
and differ only in whether every label calibrates (full CP), $n$
leave-one-out folds calibrate (jackknife+), or a train and calibration split
calibrates (split CP).

\input{figs/table_attrib_a01.tex}

Jackknife+ reproduces FRCP set for set. Its mean set size and marginal
coverage match to within standard error in every one of the forty cells, with
a largest size difference of $0.09$ of a label, so the per-candidate
transductive refit contributes nothing measurable to efficiency. Full CP's
distinct value over jackknife+ is therefore the exact $1-\alpha$ guarantee at
the same cost, not tighter sets, since jackknife+ carries only the
$1-2\alpha$ bound while covering at the target in practice here. Split
conformal with the same score pays for the split: it loses at two shots
($2.69$ against $2.43$ on CIFAR-100, $4.28$ against $3.59$ on EuroSAT) and
converges to FRCP by eight to fourteen shots, yet it never collapses the way
the trained-head baselines do, returning $2.69$ rather than $92.9$ labels on
CIFAR-100 at two shots. Most of FRCP's margin over those baselines is thus the
training-free score, which keeps split CP usable, and the residual small
margin is full CP spending every label on calibration. At $\alpha = 0.05$ and
two shots on the ten-class datasets, split CP hits the full-set floor
(Table~\ref{tab:attrib-a005}): after the train split $n_{\mathrm{cal}}$ falls
below the $\lceil 1/\alpha\rceil - 1$ labels a quantile at that level needs, a
finite-sample floor full CP escapes.

\section{Score-level versus representation-level smoothing}
\label{app:snaps}

Section~\ref{sec:related} promises a direct comparison of the two levels at
which a pool correction can act. Table~\ref{tab:snaps-vs-smoothing} makes it,
holding the construction fixed to the static-calibration form of full CP so
the arms differ only in the smoothing level. We confirmed on one cell that
this construction reproduces the full-CP sets set for set, on $100\%$ of test
points.

\input{figs/table_snaps_vs_smoothing.tex}

The representation-level smoothing
stage recovers more of the truncation-to-refinement gain than the score-level
SNAPS correction at the small budgets where refinement matters: at two shots
it cuts the CIFAR-100 set from $3.61$ to $2.44$ against SNAPS's $2.82$, and the
miniImageNet set from $1.38$ to $1.23$ against $1.28$, and the two converge by
eight shots. Stacking adds essentially nothing over smoothing alone ($2.41$
against $2.44$ at two shots on CIFAR-100). The two levels cost comparable
per-trial wall-clock ($0.37$ s each against $0.10$ s for the base at eight
shots on CIFAR-100), because the score-level correction must re-score the
whole pool under each new calibration set, so smoothing is at least as good on
efficiency at no greater cost. The leave-one-out SNAPS correction holds marginal coverage at or
above the target in every cell, its lowest sitting exactly at the $0.900$
target, so the under-coverage a naive per-candidate averaging would incur
does not appear;
its softer coupling to the labels is still not the exact per-candidate
recomputation, so it is reported as measured and its $\eta$ and $k$ are not
tuned on the roster.
