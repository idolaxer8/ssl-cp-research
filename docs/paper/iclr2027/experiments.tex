\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\textbf{Datasets.} We evaluate on four standard image-classification
benchmarks: CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100~\citep{krizhevsky2009learning},
\emph{mini}ImageNet~\citep{vinyals2016matching}, and
EuroSAT~\citep{helber2019eurosat}. The first three are consumer
photographs of the kind the backbone saw in pre-training, while EuroSAT
is Sentinel-2 satellite imagery and sits further from it.
Embeddings come from two frozen encoders, DINOv2
ViT-B/14~\citep{oquab2023dinov2} and CLIP ViT-B~\citep{radford2021clip},
both of dimension $d = 768$, and every comparison is run on each. For
each dataset the unlabeled pool $\mathcal{U}$ holds 10,000 held-out
images of the same distribution and never overlaps the labeled sample.
\textbf{Configuration.} One configuration serves every dataset and both
encoders. Truncation keeps $d' = 128$ dimensions, whitening pools the within-group covariance over
$C = K$ $k$-means groups, and smoothing averages each embedding over
$\kappa = 10$ pool neighbors with weight exponent $\gamma = 3$. The
score is the prototype softmax of \eqref{eq:score}. Its temperature $T$
is the one quantity resolved per dataset and encoder, fixed once from a
small pilot split and then held constant across every trial and budget.
Appendix~B reports the values.
\textbf{Baselines.} We compare against split CP on a trained linear
head, scored by the three standard conformal
scores~\citep{sadinle2019least, romano2020aps, angelopoulos2021raps},
SemiCP~\citep{zhou2026semicp}, which augments calibration with
nearest-neighbor-matched unlabeled scores, and
CV+~\citep{barber2021predictive}.
% probe+CP row pending g3 (R2): selftrain / pool-MLP reviewer arms.
Every split-based baseline divides its labels between training and
calibration. We grant each its most favorable allocation per cell,
selected with hindsight from $n_{\mathrm{tr}}/n \in \{0.25, 0.5,
0.75\}$, and its best score, so all comparisons favor the baselines.
SemiCP follows the official implementation(adaptations in Appendix~E).
The unlabeled pool is available to every method that can consume one.
\textbf{Protocol.} The labeled budget is indexed by shots per class
$s = n/K$, on a grid $s \in \{2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14\}$. Each cell
averages 50 independent trials that resample the labeled sample, the
test points (5 per class), and where applicable the baseline's
train/calibration split. Labeled samples are class-balanced.
% Random-split arm DROPPED 08-30 (user call). Tables survive at
% output/r1_headline/plots/table_r1b_random_*.tex if it returns.
We report $\alpha = 0.1$ in the main text and $\alpha = 0.05$ in
Appendix~C, with three metrics: marginal coverage, mean prediction-set
size, and
the class-conditional
coverage gap
$\mathrm{CovGap} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_c |\mathrm{cov}_c - (1-\alpha)|$ in
percentage points, reported in Appendix~F.

\textbf{Below the boundary, spending every label on calibration wins.}
At $s = 2$ split CP and SemiCP return 63 to 98 percent of the $K$
candidates on every dataset and encoder, while FRCP returns 1.0 to 3.6
labels and the smallest set in all eight cells. CV+ is the only
competitive alternative there, at 1.3 to 3.6 times the FRCP size and
under the weaker $1 - 2\alpha$ guarantee. Across the two tables FRCP is
smallest in 24 of 32 cells.
\textbf{Set sizes are comparable only because coverage is.} FRCP holds
marginal coverage at or above the target in all 32 cells, between
0.900 and 0.948. SemiCP falls below it in four cells on each encoder,
reaching 0.870 on EuroSAT under CLIP, so part of the size advantage it
shows at larger budgets is bought back from coverage rather than
earned on efficiency.
\textbf{The advantage narrows as the budget grows, sooner on the weaker
encoder.} On DINOv2 FRCP is still smallest on all four datasets at
$s = 8$, and by $s = 14$ CV+ passes it on \emph{mini}ImageNet and
EuroSAT, with the margins on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 down to a few
percent. On CLIP the crossover arrives earlier, at $s = 4$ on EuroSAT
and $s = 8$ on CIFAR-100 and \emph{mini}ImageNet, while CIFAR-10 holds
to $s = 14$. CLIP sets are larger throughout, on CIFAR-100 by more than
four times at the largest budget, 5.26 against 1.22. Neither shift
reaches the small-budget ranking, where CV+ remains the strongest
baseline in every cell on both encoders. Appendix~\ref{app:backbones}
reports the full grids at both miscoverage levels.
\textbf{Runtime.} Conformalizing the 500-point CIFAR-100 test set takes
0.4 seconds at $s = 2$ and 1.3 at $s = 14$ on one GPU, after a one-off
8.4 seconds to fit $T_{\mathcal{U}}$. Split CP needs 0.2 and 0.7,
CV+ 0.2 and 3.2, so beyond the smallest budgets the closed-form update
of \eqref{eq:score} makes full CP the cheaper of the two.
\textbf{Full CP or the score?} Every baseline differs from FRCP in both
the construction and the score, so Appendix~\ref{app:attrib} varies only
the construction. Jackknife+ with the same score reproduces FRCP set for
set, so full CP earns the exact guarantee rather than efficiency. Split
CP with the same score loses at two shots and converges by eight without
collapsing, which places most of the margin on the training-free score
and the rest on spending every label on calibration.

\textbf{Refinement pays most at small budgets, and only the full chain
delivers it.} The full pipeline returns the smallest sets in all 12
dataset and budget cells of Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}. On CIFAR-100 it
reduces set size by 79\% at $s = 2$ (10.7 $\to$ 2.27), by 36\% at
$s = 4$, and by 11\% at $s = 8$. No single stage gets below 4.16 there
at two shots. Truncation and whitening together deliver the largest
step, and smoothing removes up to a further third (3.35 $\to$ 2.27).

\textbf{Order matters, not just membership.} Whitening alone
destabilizes the temperature fit, which collapses to its floor on every
dataset, and its sets inflate past the raw embedding on EuroSAT at all
three budgets (6.40 against 5.52 at $s = 2$). With truncation first the
fit is stable and the pair improves on either stage alone on three of
the four datasets. EuroSAT only breaks even at the pair, and there
smoothing lands the decisive step (4.32 $\to$ 3.65 at $s = 2$), which
is the ordering Section~\ref{sec:refine} fixes.

\textbf{Refinement separates the score mass around the quantile.}
Refinement concentrates the true-class scores below the calibration
quantile and pushes false-class mass above it, so the same coverage is
bought with fewer false labels in the set: the leak falls from 3.03 to
0.11 on CIFAR-10 and from 2.68 to 0.78 on CIFAR-100 at essentially
unchanged coverage. Even on EuroSAT, where the two masses stay
entangled, the leak drops from 4.05 to 1.72.

\textbf{Smoothing the representation, not the score.}
Appendix~\ref{app:snaps} compares the two levels at which a pool
correction can act (Table~\ref{tab:snaps-vs-smoothing}): the
representation-level smoothing stage recovers more of the refinement
gain than a score-level SNAPS correction at the small budgets that
matter, at comparable per-trial cost, and stacking the two adds little.
