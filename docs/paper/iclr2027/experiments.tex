\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Datasets.} We evaluate on four image-classification
benchmarks chosen to span a range of difficulty for the frozen
backbone. CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100~\citep{krizhevsky2009learning} and
\emph{mini}ImageNet~\citep{vinyals2016matching} sit at the easier end,
with classes the embedding separates readily.
EuroSAT~\citep{helber2019eurosat} sits at the harder end, since it
moves the backbone off its pretraining domain: its images are
Sentinel-2 satellite tiles rather than consumer photographs.
Class counts range from ten to a hundred, so the label budget rather
than the label space sets the regime. The reference backbone is a
frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14~\citep{oquab2023dinov2} ($d = 768$), and
Section~\ref{sec:headline} repeats every comparison on a second frozen
encoder, CLIP ViT-B.
For each dataset the unlabeled pool $\mathcal{U}$ is drawn from held-out
images of the same distribution and never overlaps the labeled sample.

\textbf{Configuration.} Following Section~\ref{sec:refine}, FRCP is
run per dataset at a fixed configuration: which refinement stages are
active, the truncation dimension $d'$, the number of $k$-means groups
$C$, the neighborhood size $\kappa$ and weight exponent $\gamma$, and
the score together with its temperature $T$. Each is fixed before the
reported runs and held constant across every trial and budget. All four
datasets use the prototype softmax score of
\eqref{eq:score}. Appendix~B lists the settings
per dataset and the sweeps behind them.
% Per-dataset values must be re-verified against the results JSONs
% before they are written into Appendix B (v4 note still open). The
% wording "fixed before the reported runs" is deliberate: T is fitted
% on a labeled pilot split (resolve_softmax_T), not from pool
% statistics alone.

\textbf{Baselines.} We compare against split CP with THR, APS, and RAPS
scores on a trained linear head~\citep{sadinle2019least, romano2020aps,
angelopoulos2021raps}, SemiCP~\citep{zhou2026semicp}, which augments
calibration with nearest-neighbor-matched unlabeled scores, and
CV+~\citep{barber2021predictive}.
% probe+CP row pending g3 (R2): selftrain / pool-MLP reviewer arms.
Every split-based baseline divides its labels between training and
calibration. We grant each its most favorable allocation per cell,
selected with hindsight from $n_{\mathrm{tr}}/n \in \{0.25, 0.5,
0.75\}$, and SemiCP additionally its best score, so all comparisons
favor the baselines. SemiCP follows the official implementation
(adaptations in Appendix~E). The unlabeled pool is available to every
method that can consume one.

\textbf{Protocol.} The labeled budget is indexed by shots per class
$s = n/K$, on a grid $s \in \{2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14\}$. Each cell
averages 50 independent trials that resample the labeled sample, the
test points (5 per class), and where applicable the baseline's
train/calibration split. Labeled samples are class-balanced.
% Random-split arm DROPPED 08-30 (user call). Tables survive at
% output/r1_headline/plots/table_r1b_random_*.tex if it returns.
We report $\alpha = 0.1$ in the main text and $\alpha = 0.05$ in
Appendix~C, with three metrics: marginal coverage, mean prediction-set
size, and
the class-conditional
coverage gap
$\mathrm{CovGap} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_c |\mathrm{cov}_c - (1-\alpha)|$ in
percentage points, reported in Appendix~F.

\textbf{Below the boundary, spending every label on calibration wins.}
At $s = 2$ split CP and SemiCP return most of the label space on every
dataset and both encoders, from 63 to 98 percent of the $K$
candidates. FRCP returns between 1.0 and 3.6 labels per set on the
reference backbone and the smallest set in all eight dataset and
encoder cells. CV+ is the only competitive alternative at that budget,
at 1.3 to 3.6 times the FRCP set size and under the weaker
$1 - 2\alpha$ guarantee. Across the two tables FRCP returns the
smallest sets in 24 of 32 cells, including every cell at two shots.

\textbf{Set sizes are comparable only because coverage is.} FRCP holds
marginal coverage at or above the target in all 32 cells, between
0.900 and 0.948. SemiCP falls below it in four cells on each encoder,
reaching 0.870 on EuroSAT under CLIP, so part of the size advantage it
shows at larger budgets is bought back from coverage rather than
earned on efficiency.

\textbf{The advantage narrows as the budget grows, sooner on the
weaker encoder.} On DINOv2 FRCP still returns the smallest sets on all
four datasets at $s = 8$. By $s = 14$ CV+ passes it on
\emph{mini}ImageNet and on EuroSAT, and the remaining margins on
CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 are a few percent. On CLIP the crossover comes
earlier, at $s = 4$ on EuroSAT and $s = 8$ on CIFAR-100, while
CIFAR-10 holds through $s = 14$ and \emph{mini}ImageNet to $s = 8$.
The whole-budget-to-calibration trade therefore pays where the budget
is smallest and fades as it grows, which is the boundary
Section~\ref{sec:intro} locates, and that boundary arrives sooner
where the encoder separates classes less cleanly.

\textbf{Representation quality moves the absolute sizes, not the
small-budget ranking.} CLIP sets are larger across the board, on
CIFAR-100 by more than four times at the largest budget (5.26 against
1.22 at $s = 14$), yet at two shots the ranking is unchanged and CV+
is the strongest baseline in every one of those cells on both
encoders. Appendix~\ref{app:backbones} reports the full grids at both
miscoverage levels.

\textbf{Runtime.} Conformalizing the 500-point CIFAR-100 test set
takes 0.4 seconds at $s = 2$ and 1.3 seconds at $s = 14$ on one GPU,
after a one-off 8.4 seconds to fit $T_{\mathcal{U}}$ on the pool.
Split CP needs 0.2 and 0.7 seconds, since it scores against a single
fitted head. CV+ needs 0.2 and 3.2 seconds, so beyond the smallest
budgets the closed-form refit of \eqref{eq:score} makes full CP the
cheaper of the two.

\textbf{Full CP or the score?} Because every baseline differs from FRCP in
both the construction and the score, Appendix~\ref{app:attrib} adds two arms
that hold the frozen transform and score fixed and vary only the construction.
Jackknife+ with the same score reproduces FRCP set for set, so the
transductive refit earns no efficiency and full CP's value is the exact
guarantee. Split CP with the same score loses at two shots and converges by
eight without ever collapsing, which places most of the margin over the
trained-head baselines on the training-free score and the rest on spending
every label on calibration.

\textbf{Refinement pays most at small budgets, and only the full chain
delivers it.} The full pipeline returns the smallest sets in all 12
dataset and budget cells of Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}. On CIFAR-100 it
reduces set size by 79\% at $s = 2$ (10.7 $\to$ 2.27), by 36\% at
$s = 4$, and by 11\% at $s = 8$. No single stage comes close on that
dataset at two shots: truncation alone reaches 4.16, smoothing alone
4.56, and whitening alone 7.70. Truncation and whitening together
deliver the largest step, and smoothing removes up to a further third
(3.35 $\to$ 2.27).

\textbf{Order matters, not just membership.} Whitening alone
destabilizes the temperature fit, which collapses to its floor on
every dataset, and its sets inflate past the raw embedding on EuroSAT
at all three budgets (6.40 against 5.52 at $s = 2$). With truncation
first the fit is stable and the pair improves on either stage alone on
three of the four datasets. Off-domain EuroSAT only breaks even at the
pair, and there smoothing lands the decisive step (4.32 $\to$ 3.65 at
$s = 2$). The figure thus recovers, in data, the ordering
Section~\ref{sec:refine} fixes: truncation conditions the space that
whitening equalizes, and smoothing acts last.

\textbf{Refinement separates the score mass around the quantile.}
Figure~\ref{fig:score-hist} shows the mechanism the ablation
aggregates. Refinement concentrates the true-class scores below the
calibration quantile and pushes false-class mass above it, so the same
coverage is bought with fewer false labels in the set: the leak falls
from 3.03 to 0.11 on CIFAR-10 and from 2.68 to 0.78 on CIFAR-100 at
essentially unchanged coverage. Even on off-domain EuroSAT, where the
two masses stay entangled, the leak drops from 4.05 to 1.72. The pool
neighborhoods that smoothing averages over explain where that last
step is safe: whitening raises the weighted pool homophily $h_w$ on
every dataset, and the fraction of labeled points
Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth} certifies rises with it on three of the four,
reaching 52 to 68\% (Table~\ref{tab:h-cert}). EuroSAT is the one
inversion, homophily up but certificates down, and the certificate
reads at the level of the representation exactly as
Section~\ref{sec:theory} scopes it.

\textbf{Smoothing the representation, not the score.}
Appendix~\ref{app:snaps} compares the two levels at which a pool
correction can act (Table~\ref{tab:snaps-vs-smoothing}): the
representation-level smoothing stage recovers more of the refinement
gain than a score-level SNAPS correction at the small budgets that
matter, at comparable per-trial cost, and stacking the two adds
little.
